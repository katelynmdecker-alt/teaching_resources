Lesson 3: Complex Numbers

Broadening our number system for roots that never touch the x-axis.

√-1 = i

Imaginary Numbers

Crucial Arithmetic Rule

i² = -1

This rule allows us to turn imaginary expressions back into real values. Whenever you see i², swap it for -1.

The Anatomy of a Complex Number

A complex number combines a real number and an imaginary number, written in standard form:

a + bi
a The Real Part
bi The Imaginary Part

Working with Complex Numbers

Add & Subtract

Combine like terms. Add the real parts together, and the imaginary parts together.

(3 + 2i) + (4 - 5i)
= (3 + 4) + (2i - 5i)
= 7 - 3i

Multiply

Use distribution (FOIL). Crucial step: whenever you get an i², swap it for -1!

2i(4 + 3i)
= 8i + 6i²
= 8i + 6(-1) = -6 + 8i

Powers of i Cycle

The powers of i repeat in a predictable cycle of four!

i¹ = i
i² = -1
i³ = -i
i⁴ = 1

Real-World: AC Circuits

Electrical engineers use complex numbers to measure impedance (resistance to alternating current). The real part is standard resistance, and the imaginary part is reactance!

Guided Example:

Find the total impedance of two components connected in a series: Z₁ = 4 + 3i Ω and Z₂ = 2 - i Ω. (Hint: add them together)

Z_total = (4 + 3i) + (2 - i)
Z_total = (4 + 2) + (3i - i)
Z_total = 6 + 2i Ω
